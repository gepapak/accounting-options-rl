# Independent novelty review of the revised accounting-options manuscript

Research cutoff: 31 October 2026. This is Part 2 of the requested independent review, based on the current identification, prevention, related-work and discussion sections, the PMR development note, the earlier `DEEP_NOVELTY_REVIEW.md` and `DEEP_NOVELTY_LITERATURE.md`, and primary sources checked below. No study was rerun, no policy was trained, no registered file was changed, and no Git command was used. The separate theory and statistics reviews determine whether the new results are valid; the labels below do not certify them.

## Decision

The new material raises the paper above a diagnostic that failed prospective transfer: it offers a concrete reward-reconstruction problem, alternative access assumptions, implemented corrections and meaningful competing remedies. Its most defensible novelty is **specialized identification and evaluation for accounting-censored simulator rewards**, supported by a controlled empirical benchmark. It is not a new general theory of reward corruption, counterfactual learning, minimax identification, reward estimation, risk control or auditing. The exact accounting sign-pair construction and the specific favorable-sign magnitude pool were **not found as direct duplicates** in this search; that is a qualified priority finding, not a first-ever guarantee. I would call it a potentially publishable applied AI contribution after correction and compression, but **not yet an evidently strong EAAI submission on method novelty alone**.

The strongest two sentences I would defend, after the mathematical qualifications identified by the theory review are made, are:

> We formulate loss-forgiveness bookkeeping as a structured corruption of an RL simulator's reward and characterize the booked-profit information recoverable with a sign-counterfactual query, including the remaining ambiguity under complete censoring. We implement and evaluate the resulting reward corrections and a clone-free, invariant-magnitude alternative in a reproducible electricity-trading benchmark, with explicit failures and evidence that a tuned ruin penalty is a competitive alternative.

These sentences do not claim universal reward recovery, generic new estimator machinery, superiority to all cheap baselines, or validation of an inaccessible-ledger deployment that has not been performed.

## What changed relative to the previous novelty judgment

The earlier report's positive direction was a pre-training diagnostic/witness with prospective acquisition discrimination. That discrimination failed in Study R and cannot now be used to establish method superiority. The current paper instead centers a different, narrower method contribution: recovering a latent booked reward from a known structural family of censored observations. This is a legitimate change in emphasis if the failed diagnostic remains visible and the access assumptions are made precise. Adding more trained agents does not by itself strengthen the priority claim.

Three particularly relevant antecedents absent from the current related-work discussion deserve attention:

* **Wang, Liu and Li (AAAI 2020)** already construct unbiased surrogate rewards from a biased corrupted channel and study learning guarantees. Their invertible confusion matrix is a real difference: a hard many-to-one floor destroys information and cannot simply be inverted.
* **Wei, Qu and Miao (ICML 2026)** explicitly identify and evaluate policy value with rewards missing not at random, including censoring, using a future-state shadow variable and a bridge estimator. Their positivity and completeness assumptions differ from an unknown deterministic floor and fail in completely unobserved regions. Nonetheless, generic “evaluation without the true reward channel” is established.
* **Lazzati and Metelli (ICLR 2026)** explicitly connect feedback-specific feasible reward sets, minimax decisions, and gaining identifiability through either more feedback or more structural assumptions. The general principle behind an access ladder and a two-world regret example is already known.

The manuscript already acknowledges Tobit-style censoring, exogenous counterfactuals, difference rewards and limited liability. Those acknowledgments are appropriate, but citing only these broad antecedents understates the nearer reward-correction and incomplete-reward evaluation literature.

### Search limits and negative findings

Searches covered combinations of censored/truncated/floored rewards, Tobit, corrupted channels, reward estimation, magnitude/sign pooling, antithetic and counterfactual rewards, exogenous MDPs, missing-reward OPE, ruin/limited liability, and metamorphic/accounting audits. Publisher records, arXiv full texts, author manuscripts and original software issues were prioritized, including 2025–2026 work. A direct implementation of the present favorable-sign, cost-normalized PMR under an unknown lower threshold was not found. Neither was the exact stated `4/9` accounting example. The absence of those phrases is weak priority evidence; the generic mathematics underneath them is established.

The exact two-world bound is especially modest: with signal advantage `delta`, per-unit cost `c`, action cap `Q`, and hidden magnitudes `m_L,m_H` such that `delta*m_L<c<delta*m_H`, put `A=c-delta*m_L`, `B=delta*m_H-c`. A signal-aligned leverage `a` has regrets `A*a` and `B*(Q-a)`. Equalizing them gives `a=Q*B/(A+B)` and minimax regret `Q*A*B/(A+B)`. The manuscript's `m_L=1,m_H=10,delta=.2,c=Q=1` gives `4/9`. This is a helpful concrete application of an indistinguishability argument, not a new general RL lower-bound technique.

## Mathematical qualifications that affect novelty claims

These are proposed meaning-changing corrections, not edits made to source files.

1. In `sections/identification.tex`, the paragraph after Proposition `prop:twin` and Table `tab:position` call SPR the sharp lower endpoint. That is incompatible with the preceding exact identification result when rewards differ. Distinguish a universally valid lower bound on magnitude from the lower endpoint conditional on the complete observed pair and Assumption C.
2. Proposition `prop:pooling` conflates a finite realized pool with its population mean. An uncensored one-draw pool can lie below `E[m]`. The upward inequality is pointwise between each transformed draw and its own `m`, and therefore between corresponding sample averages; comparison with the population mean needs expectation, convergence or a probability bound. With the true mean, the specified conditional unbiasedness may hold under the needed joint conditional independence. A running estimate is not automatically conditionally unbiased in a state that is itself influenced by previous rewards and actions.
3. M alone does not exclude all fully censored worlds with different global invariant magnitude laws. To identify the law by pooling, the data must actually provide informative favorable observations or a bound/correction on their censoring inflation. Say what permits distinguishing the worlds, not merely that the law is invariant across states.
4. The V arm `r-lambda*r^2` is exactly a quadratic-utility/second-moment penalty. Its expectation differs from `E[r]-lambda*Var(r)` by `lambda*(E[r])^2`. Label the implemented surrogate precisely. Its loss of legitimate edge does not establish that risk-sensitive RL in general loses that edge.
5. `sections/discussion.tex` says the working ruin strength “can be identified only from booked returns of trained agents.” That is too strong: held-out twin-audited returns, observable ruin constraints, or conservative selection rules are possible under their respective access assumptions. Whether they work here requires evaluation. A trainer already possessing a twin could instead train TIR, so audit-based penalty selection is an access/performance comparison, not an automatic necessity argument for twins.

## EAAI strength and likely rejection arguments

The [official EAAI scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/engineering-applications-of-artificial-intelligence/0952-1976) includes verification/validation, safety, diagnostics and engineering case studies, while asking for novel AI aspects and reproducible public-data validation. A new PPO optimizer is therefore unnecessary. But the abstract must identify an AI contribution distinct from the market application, and an enormous sequence of toy-sleeve studies cannot substitute for a convincing use of the proposed audit.

* An **RL theorist** could reject an inflated theorem story: simple censoring algebra, a toy minimax constant, standard averaging, and assumptions that remove magnitude prediction. The answer is sharper correct scope and a useful quantitative guarantee, not more theorem names.
* An **energy-market reviewer** could reject an engineering bridge that is missing: sign-symmetric sleeve dynamics and known own cost do not automatically describe the original engine, actual settlement/liability behavior, endogenous impact or nonlinear legitimate positions. A native-engine reward-channel audit with a verified observation contract would be the most persuasive bridge.
* The **EAAI desk** could reject a 65-page main paper whose AI novelty is hard to locate, or a claim of a generally superior remedy contradicted by the tuned ruin penalty. A concise engineering-verification paper with a focused contribution is more defensible than a catalogue of studies.

**Readiness judgment:** after fixing substantive statements, the paper may have a credible EAAI route; “strong because T/U/V supported many hypotheses” is not justified. Its strongest outstanding empirical gap is whether the ledger-free method is useful in the harder simulator where the ledger is genuinely withheld, with all required access premises verified. That experiment is not already supplied by the current sleeve replay or by unrelated runtime solvency guards.

## Ranked additions: value per time/risk, at most five

These are recommendations only. None is registered or executed here. They must not repair or overwrite T/U/V. The run budgets below are alternatives; only proposal 3 requests new PPO runs, and that one stays within 24 total at at most one million steps each and at most six workers.

### 1. Finish the identification result with an honest finite-pool error theorem

**Why:** it replaces an incorrect blanket unbiasedness claim with a reusable statement specifying exactly how estimation, censoring and state dependence enter an RL reward correction. This is a modest theoretical advance in presentation/use, not a new concentration principle.

**Minimum proof:** let `F_t` contain prior pool data and actions, and require the current magnitude/sign independence conditional on the allowed information. With predictable `mhat_t`, `delta_t=E[sigma_t|F_t,a_t]` and invariant current mean `mu`, derive

`E[xhat_t-x_t | F_t,a_t] = |q_t|*delta_t*(mhat_t-mu)`.

For pooled observations `U_i=m_i+b_i`, `b_i>=0`, state separately the sampling error and average inflation. Under bounded iid or an appropriate martingale model, bound their sum; if neither censoring inflation nor the magnitude tail is bounded, prove that no uniform finite bound follows. Include the empirical realized mean versus population distinction. Generalize the two-world calculation above if retained; do not sell its numerical constant as a sample-complexity result.

**Decision rules / arms / compute:** no PPO runs; exact hand cases for zero pool, one observation, all-censored data, predictable adaptive action selection and a violated-independence example; mathematical equality must hold on the stated class and counterexamples must break any removed premise. Fixed exact enumerations, no selected favorable seeds. Existing V/T/U aggregate results do not prove this theorem; existing implementation can be checked against its filtration without training. Expected outcome is a corrected, narrower theorem. Falsification of the current broad claim is already easy; null risk for a stronger useful bound is high if no observable censoring bound exists.

### 2. Withhold the ledger and audit the actual Section 4 engine

**Why:** closes the highest-value engineering gap using already learned controllers, and tests the intended reason to use a reward-channel estimator at all.

**Minimum design:** first test a declared observation contract on hand/source cases: same state/action/cost, exact sign intervention, preserved market magnitude, invariant non-sign state, and an explicit check that the reward really has the asserted censored form. An unsupported contract is a failed feasibility result; do not change the simulator to force C and call it native validation. If feasible, freeze all controller/case selections, compare reported, SPR, TIR and PMR estimates against a separately retained hidden booked trace, and include clean plus documented faulty accounting versions.

**Hypotheses / decision rules:** H1 held-out absolute episode audit error is at most a predeclared engineering tolerance; H2 the audit distinguishes economically losing proxy winners from profitable or neutral controls without false positives on clean controls; H3 every estimator's access contract holds. Require H3 for interpreting H1/H2. Report every failure and abstention. Use paired episode differences and cluster by market episode/controller, not native timesteps.

**Arms / seeds / compute:** zero new PPO training, all six already frozen PPO seeds/variants if available and the strongest fixed learned/analytic controller, using a locked existing case roster. At most one extra counterfactual native step per actual step for twin methods. Existing engine and saved models permit a source/coupling feasibility look; V/T/U sleeve aggregates do not answer H3. Expected result is uncertain because the native reward may violate C or the cost/symmetry premises. A contract failure would be scientifically useful but would not establish the advertised deployment benefit.

### 3. Deliberately violate M, and test a conditional magnitude estimate

**Why:** addresses the method's largest untested assumption instead of adding another seed in a construction that enforces it. It can show a useful boundary and, if justified, a narrowly extended method.

**Minimum experiment:** two observable volatility regimes with known different magnitude laws, one persistent enough to affect equity/state; keep a sign signal but arrange costs so the honest optimal action genuinely differs by regime and is not always the cap. Fit/pool magnitude causally by regime, freeze burn-in and censoring handling, and measure conditional reward bias and action choices separately from aggregate return. A conditional pool is standard estimation; novelty is not obtained merely by adding a regressor.

**Hypotheses / decision rules:** H1 unconditional PMR fails a predeclared conditional-bias tolerance in the constructed violation; H2 conditional PMR restores that tolerance where the relevant uncensored information is available; H3 it meets both exploit and edge-retention criteria against booked training in each regime. Require all three for the claimed extension; no promotion of an alternate regressor if H2/H3 fail.

**Arms / seeds / compute:** PLAIN, booked, unconditional PMR and conditional PMR; six common seeds each, 24 exploratory PPO runs, at most 1,000,000 steps/run and six workers. Perform exact/toy policy calculations before any training. TIR can be evaluated as a reward-estimation diagnostic without adding a fifth training arm. Current V/T/U magnitudes deliberately satisfy M, so existing results cannot falsify its robustness; no honest resampling of their aggregate scores creates this test. Expected unconditional failure is designed, but successful conditional correction is uncertain under censoring and poor regime coverage. High null risk for H3; do not spend the budget if an exact analysis already shows that the current reward-only inputs cannot identify the conditional means.

### 4. Select a ruin strength using held-out twin-audited return

**Why:** directly resolves the unnecessary “only booked returns can tune it” claim and turns an awkward competitive baseline into an informative access/cost comparison.

**Minimum analysis:** use the three existing ruin strengths, lock a two-way split of the 100 audit episodes, select by TIR-audited edge subject to the declared exploit/ruin criterion on one half, evaluate on the other half, and swap halves. Retain paired training seeds and common episode clustering. Compare the selected rule with fixed lambda 5, oracle booked-return selection as an upper reference, and direct TIR training where available. PMR audit selection must be cross-fitted; its current pooled evaluation estimate should not be treated as an independent causal prediction.

**Hypotheses / decision rules:** audit selection agrees with the booked selection within a prespecified return tolerance on held-out episodes and meets the same two success criteria; otherwise report it as unreliable. Do not infer fresh confirmatory success from a split chosen after looking at outcomes.

**Arms / seeds / compute:** zero PPO runs, eight existing matched seeds per A/C arm, all 100 saved episode returns. `run_v.py` saves `audit_edge/episodes` and `audit_placebo/episodes`, so a first descriptive analysis is cheap. Reusing the same test weeks makes this exploratory, not independent generalization. Expected selection could recover the competitive penalty, which would strengthen the comparative account but reduce any claim that twin training is necessary. It is partially circular in access: the same clone could simply train TIR; the useful question is accuracy/cost/performance of selection, not a new reward method.

### 5. A second public domain — defer until a genuine matching mechanism is located

**Why:** a native second domain would substantially improve generality; a fabricated floor around CartPole or inventory is another synthetic mechanism demonstration.

**Feasibility:** FinRL is publicly available, but its spot-cash affordability constraint and known terminal reward issue are not automatically an instance of `max(sigma*g-C,tau)`, and the two original issue reports above already establish priority for those bugs. Inventory lost-sales systems naturally censor demand, but the payoff is not a sign-linear gross move with a known own-cost subtraction; blindly applying TIR/PMR would be unjustified. Public existence alone is not compatibility.

**Minimum proof/decision rule:** before any training, identify a native lower-censoring reward contract, observable sign and own costs, and a valid sign intervention; reject the transfer claim if any is absent. Then use frozen policies or exhaustive small policies to compare estimator errors and clean/faulty accounting. Require a prespecified tolerance and retain all failures.

**Arms / seeds / compute:** zero PPO runs before feasibility; fixed enumerated action paths or ten declared rollouts per relevant hand policy. If a true matching domain is found, it deserves a separate powered design, not an improvised slice of the remaining laptop budget. Existing V/T/U supply no second-domain evidence. Expected outcome before submission is likely a feasibility boundary, with high null/engineering risk. **I would not add second-domain training now.**

## Title and framing

“Sign-Twin Rewards Prevent It” is too narrow for a story that now includes a clone-free method and a working cheap penalty, while “Every Accounting Rule Is an Option” is too broad unless clearly restricted to the paper's ladder. At most three alternatives:

1. **Loss Forgiveness in Reinforcement-Learning Trading: Accounting Bias, Reward Identification and Prevention** — recommended. Covers the mechanism, identification and alternatives without promising universal twin superiority.
2. **Learning from Censored Trading Rewards: Accounting Exploits and Ledger-Free Corrections** — use only after the reward-reconstruction scope is corrected; “ledger-free” still needs explicit cost/sign/clone access in the abstract.
3. **When Trading Simulators Forgive Losses: Identifying and Correcting Reinforcement-Learning Incentives** — emphasizes engineering validation and permits the competitive penalty result.

No title or manuscript framing was changed in this review.
