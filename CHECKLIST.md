# Evaluation checklist for RL trading agents and their simulators

For anyone who trains or accepts a learned trading controller on the basis of simulated performance, and for anyone who sets the settlement rules a trader operates under. Each item points to the evidence in this repository that shows why it matters.

## Accounting
- [ ] **Declare the liability rule.** State how the simulator books losses beyond equity, how positions are sized (fixed allocation or fraction of equity), and what happens at bankruptcy, alongside every reported result. A zero floor or a capped final loss is a modelling decision with behavioral consequences, not a safeguard.
  - Under a zero floor, agents learned to take maximal leverage near zero equity: 11.1 leverage units of 16 above full-liability agents (`LIABILITY_STUDY_2026-09-29/results_f/`).
- [ ] **Never floor cash silently; book the debt.** Either end the episode on ruin with the full loss booked, or book any recapitalization as debt in the reward and in the reported result. Real settlement systems require collateral precisely because a distressed party may stop paying.
  - Capping only the final loss taught far less gambling than a floor that keeps trading (Studies B, F, I, J).
- [ ] **Price the option your accounting writes.** Full liability, a capped final loss, a settlement lag, terminal limited liability and a zero floor form one ladder of increasing forgiveness. Under a settlement lag, insolvent but undetected agents took maximal leverage at every probe when their deficit would be forgiven (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_n/`).
- [ ] **Use a calibrated pre-training gap diagnostic where a reduced model is defensible.** The binned dynamic program gives an optimal-model benchmark. Its positive leverage-16 versus flat contrast is averaged over a fixed initial-equity reference grid relative to one-period noise, not actual training occupancy. O's previously fixed threshold classified 5/6 confident treatments; local gaps tied its prospective AUC. Calibration is specific to the learner, budget and model. At 90% charging, 0/10 PPO runs acquired an optimal exploit at one million steps, versus 8/10 at four million (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_o/`, `study_p/`). Neither a small index nor a short null run certifies safety.
- [ ] **If the account keeps trading after losses, charge every injection in full.** Under the no-edge assumptions this restores non-positive expected raw monetary reward. No fixed partial charge guarantees that uniformly across those markets; finite training and reward normalization remain separate issues. The matched-continuation contrast cut low-equity leverage by 9.6 units (`revision_experiments/2026-10-10/`).
- [ ] **If the ledger is not exposed, try a direct calculation first.** With the market input, the position and the payoff formula, the booked P&L is position × move − own cost: exact, with no clone, and its gap to the reward exposes every credit. It is a second implementation of the payoff, so it is wrong wherever that implementation differs from the simulator's.
- [ ] **Read the reward diagram at a sample of states.** On a clone, freeze the state and the action and move the market input along the line through the realized move. An affine reward certifies the range it covers; a convex kink is a floor, a concave kink a cap, a jump a reward replaced on a branch. Compare each with what the simulator is documented to do. A ledger shares the simulator's configuration and cannot do this: in the engine audit the reward alone separates the capped and uncapped payoffs that the audit confused (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/twin_reward/reward_diagram.py`, `exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-06/engine_scan/`). The reader needs a clone, control of the series the payoff reads and its reference, and a declared own cost; it does not apply as stated to a logarithmic reward or a convex contract, and the range is what the simulator admits.
- [ ] **If the payoff is internal, changes between versions or is not trusted as a formula, consider a sign-twin reward.** Clone the simulator before each step, reverse the exogenous sign in the clone, and train on the twin-identified reward (TIR: exact where the pair of rewards differs, `-own cost` where it coincides) or on SPR, `(r - r_twin)/2 - own cost`. Check conditional sign fairness for unbiasedness, linearity of the legitimate payoff in that sign and known own costs; where a carried deficit exceeds every attainable booked profit no such query identifies it. Studies S, T and U met their registered exploit-prevention and edge-retention criteria for both rewards in three edge markets and in two carried-deficit environments; none of three KL and five ORPO strengths met both edge-market criteria (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_s/`, `study_t/`, `study_u/`; code and tests in `twin_reward/`). With real signs in one zone (Study RS) TIR met the retention rule and SPR did not, so prefer TIR where the cost-shift test passes; the head-to-head difference was not significant. Where a carried deficit hides the gross both remove the exploit and much of the edge, 45% with half of the training in deficit and nearly all of it with all of it (Study FT), so read the diagram first. On the engine's own paths TIR's audit failed where a deficit is forgiven on both branches (median error 608 points) and SPR's where one branch is floored (618), and only the bracket held on both, so report the bracket as well (`exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/engine_twin/`). If the simulator cannot be cloned but the per-unit move size is independent of equity, action and sign, the pooled-magnitude reward needs only the action, the exogenous sign and the own cost (Study V: it removed the exploit in 31 of 32 agents). A lump-sum ruin penalty is a competitive cheap baseline only at a well-chosen strength (5% of the allocation did both in Study V; 1% left the gamble, 25% stopped trading from zero equity; in Study W's two further settings the best strength was 5% in one and 1% in the other, and 25% failed in the weaker-edge sleeve) and only if it sees every floor (next item); the quadratic-utility reward `r - lambda r^2` removed the edge with the gamble; choose any such strength on booked returns or on the ledger-free audit (`study_v/`, `study_w/`). A trained agent's booked return can be audited without the ledger by summing the twin estimates over its evaluation episodes: the error was below 0.4 points for every single-floor agent of Studies V, W and Y (240 PPO and A2C agents, each agent's mean over 100 test weeks, edge and placebo markets), against a median reported-return error of 68 points for Study V's 28 agents that gambled, and up to 1.6 points for agents that gambled under a two-write floor (Study X; `exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-03/audit_cohorts.py`). Where agents hardly gamble the reported return is also close and picks the same penalty strength (Study W, W4 not supported), so the audit matters where an agent may have gambled. Where the single-floor assumption is in doubt, report the twin bracket `[2*SPR - r, r]` summed over episodes (`twin_reward.twin_bracket`): it needs credits that are non-negative at every step on both branches and an own cost known and equal on both (the settlement lag's zombie window and a charge on one branch only break it; `exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/bracket_limits.py`), held in all 60,800 stored test episodes, on every audited episode of Studies Z and RS, on the engine's own cash writes and, in a replay of those writes, on every step of 93 evaluations of its frozen controllers (on real clones it misses where the own cost differs between the branches), and its width is the credit on the real and twin branches together, an upper bound on what the agent collected (1.35 to 2.42 times the realized credit in Studies Z and RS).
- [ ] **Measure ruin where the losses are floored.** If losses are floored per account, per sub-account or per isolated position, a penalty on the ruin of the portfolio or of the balance can miss the exploit: one account can gamble from zero while the portfolio is never ruined, and separate floors forgive at least what one pooled floor forgives. In Study Z a ruin penalty of 5% or 25% of the allocation on the portfolio or the balance left the exploit in place; measured per account it removed it and kept 79% of the edge, measured per liquidation it removed it but kept 36%, and SPR and TIR from the portfolio's reward channel removed it and kept 0.99 to 1.15 of the ledger-trained edge without knowing the account structure (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_z/`, `study_zb/`).
- [ ] **Test the single-floor assumption before using a twin reward.** Shift the own cost over a grid and check, at one state and draw, whether any single-floor world (one sign, one magnitude, one threshold) reproduces every twin pair (`twin_reward/`: `cost_shift_pairs` and `cost_shift_test`, which decides this exactly; the experiments are in `exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-02/contract_test.py` and `exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-03/exact_cost_shift/`). A twin pair alone cannot reveal a second floor, and passing is not a certificate: a violation that a single floor could have produced (a hidden fixed fee on a censored loss branch, an inflated gross credit) is invisible to any reward-only test. Read the order of the simulator's cash writes as well. The test flagged none of 5,122 states of the single-floor sleeve, all 761 informative states of a two-write variant in which the fee exceeded the account, and the engine's own source methods from starting cash 0 and 5 DKK, not 100 DKK. Scale its tolerance to the account: where rewards are differences of balances near 1e9, the default fired on rounding (in 26 of 39 engine runs with nothing forgiven, against 2 when scaled). In that two-write sleeve SPR and TIR still prevented the exploit (Study X: 0 of 16 agents gambled against 8 of 8 with the reported reward) and kept the edge, but the ledger-free audit of agents that gambled was less accurate (median TIR error 1.4 points).
- [ ] **Bound what a substitute reward can cost.** For SPR or TIR the booked regret of the trained policy is at most the conditional-bias terms along the booked-optimal policy and along the trained one (they depend on the hidden magnitude where the pair coincides, so evaluate them where the ledger is known) (`exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-02/regret_bounds.py`; the proposition on reward substitutes). Along the sleeve's honest policy TIR's term was at most 0.004% of the booked edge, SPR's 0.3 to 3.8%, and the reported reward's credit 1.2 to 19.9%.
- [ ] **Reconcile reported equity with booked P&L** for every run, and report the largest gap (`tools/ledger_check.py`).
  - Choose a numerical tolerance appropriate to the ledger, frequency and currency units. The engine audit's registered reconciliation check flagged 53 of 54 affected runs and none of 246 others (`results/`); its threshold is not a universal accounting tolerance.
- [ ] **State whether position size scales with equity, and report ruin** (the share of runs whose booked equity reaches zero) alongside returns.

## Tests
- [ ] **Run a placebo market** (`LIABILITY_STUDY_2026-09-29/placebo_market.py`). Train and evaluate the agent on the same data with the sign of the payoff randomized independently in every period. No policy can profit there in expectation, so any positive expected reported return is a false positive.
  - Floor-trained agents reported +38% to +148% in the placebo market while booking −15% to −57% (`LIABILITY_STUDY_2026-09-29/results/`).
  - In the constant-position diffusion calculation with a zero start, five draws flag false profit about 90% of the time (Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 of the paper). An agent evaluated from a full allocation reaches the floor in only some draws: use enough draws to assess that incidence, and retain the trained agent as the unit of inference.
- [ ] **Probe the equity response.** Set the equity input of a trained agent to a low and a normal value and compare the risk it takes. The probe needs no access to the simulator's accounting; it is a screening signal, not proof. Report the exploit's shape (leverage at low minus at high equity), not only a count at one probe: where honest actions are nearly indifferent, the count alone has a seed-dependent floor.
- [ ] **Look for a feasible accounting-incentive witness before training.** In an independent fair-sign model, integrate the expected one-action reward and booked cost, and compare with staying flat under the same account. A positive proxy reward with negative expected booked profit proves an available incentive for that policy. The exact one-step and ledger/clock audits in `exploratory/exploratory_novelty_2026-10-15/` supply examples; a failed witness does not certify safety, and a calibrated acquisition score is a separate heuristic. Conditional empirical precision does not establish future-market calibration. Study R's completed 60-agent cross-cost acquisition test failed its single primary composite (p=.8125), despite both full-forgiveness anchors being maximal in 10/10 runs. That failure does not invalidate the separate accounting-witness certificates.
- [ ] **Search for hidden forgiveness with a bracket-width finder.** Train an agent whose reward is the twin bracket's width, `r + r_twin + 2 * own cost` (the credit paid on the pair), and inspect where it collects credit; it needs neither the ledger nor the accounting rules. In Study Z it found an isolated-margin channel hidden from the trainer in all eight seeds (430 points of the allocation in credit per week against 17 for ledger training; `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_zb/`). Treat this as preliminary: it was tested in one authored setting without a negative control, and a payoff convex in the sign also produces width with nothing forgiven. What it finds is a witness, not a certificate: a finder that collects no credit does not show that the accounting forgives nothing.
- [ ] **Check adaptive continuations when isolated actions fail.** A negative prefix can reach a profitable low-ledger or clock state. Freeze an executable feedback policy and cap using discovery data, then confirm on independent IID whole paths. Under explicit no-edge assumptions, the accounting residual has a cost-only lower bound; a positive one-sided capped-residual bound plus strictly positive expected executed costs certifies charged gain with booked harm. Otherwise abstain. The selected-case 99% result in `exploratory/exploratory_novelty_2026-10-15/adaptive_witness_empirical/` is policy/model-specific, not a benchmark discovery-rate or acquisition claim. Use the terminal deadline compiler only with its stationarity, no-wait-information and no-early-closure assumptions; do not transfer it to step-floor accounts.
- [ ] **Test sensitivity to simulator assumptions.** Accept a learned controller only if its advantage over the rule survives the removal of each shortcut, and treat an advantage that appears only in markets without a real edge with particular suspicion (`GENERALIZATION_STUDY_2026-09-29/`).

## Market realism
- [ ] **Compare acquisition scores with cost-aware summaries on equal terms.** Include dose, executed cost relative to payoff noise, a dose/cost/risk ratio, and local or moment-based gaps. Calibrate on the same historical labels, report all equally optimal cutoff intervals, and evaluate budgets/learners separately. The completed-study comparison in `exploratory/exploratory_novelty_2026-10-15/cost_baselines/` does not establish better prediction by the integrated diagnostic. A feasible accounting witness answers a different question: the matched-moment example shows that cost/variance/absolute-payoff summaries can conceal opposite one-action incentives. Do not promote that example to a learning prediction or safety certificate.
- [ ] **Model realistic trading costs.** Under Study H's tested fee, liquidity cap, price impact and training budget, the equity-only agents did not learn the predicted low-equity exploit (`LIABILITY_STUDY_2026-09-29/results_h/`). This does not show that frictions remove every accounting incentive.
- [ ] **Cap executed volume by executable market volume**, not by capital, and report the ratio of desired to executed volume.
  - Without a cap, one rule's return grew ×300–1,000 while a learned policy was ruined (engine audit).
- [ ] **Model solvency** (margin, loss exit or a budget constraint) for any account that can lose more than its capital.
  - A missing liquidity cap and missing solvency rules were jointly necessary for the engine audit's false positive.
- [ ] **Settle on the actual payoff** of the traded product and **respect information timing**: no interpolation between future prices, no normalization statistics fitted on test data, and gate-closure times that match the market.

- [ ] **Stress collateral components against exposure and the spread distribution under explicit assumptions.** A historical volume-term proxy was below a modeled three-week buffer in 36.8% of post-reform FI/SE/NO zone-months versus 8.6% before (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/`). The model assumes randomized signs, exposure equal to consumption and an external owner cost-of-risk hurdle. The invoice term, minima and monitoring are excluded; the historical formula applied after Finland's November 2024 change is counterfactual. Include current national rules and all collateral components before judging actual adequacy. The comparison identifies neither a causal reform effect nor real exploitation.

## Statistics and reporting
- [ ] **Select and rank on the booked ledger**, not on reported equity. No metric of reported equity identifies booked performance, and more evaluation draws do not repair a biased target: winners selected on reported return were certified worst in expected booked return (`revision_experiments/2026-10-10-selection/`).
- [ ] **Report every seed, and the median alongside the mean**, together with per-seed exposure and direction. Under zero-floor accounting the mean (+270%) and the interquartile mean (+73%) of the engine's legacy protocol as run were fooled; the median (−101%) was not.
- [ ] **Annualize risk metrics at the decision frequency** and separate investor-level from fund-level metrics.
- [ ] **Read the settings the simulator actually ran with.** Record them from the running environment at its first step and compare them with the protocol's description. In the engine audit a flag removed from the command line was restored from the checkpoint's saved configuration, so a payoff described as capped ran uncapped, and the ranking it produced disappears with the cap on. Checks that compare numbers with numbers do not catch this (`exploratory/exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/engine_twin/dump_runtime_config.py`).
- [ ] **Pre-register protocol changes and report failed predictions.** Retain every gate of a composite endpoint. In completed R, the low-fee gap 4.500 passed >=4; the high-fee gap 4.475 failed <4; interaction .025 failed >=4; and the seed-cluster sign p=.8125 failed <=.05. Booked controls averaged 3.100/1.125 leverage, so a smaller raw treatment mean is not the required treatment-control interaction. The operational cutoffs do not prove population equivalence or no gambling (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_r/VERDICTS_R.md`).
- [ ] **Identify inference units and the status of each analysis.** Do not count evaluation draws or checkpoints as new trained agents. Retain matched seeds and zones in dependence sensitivity, disclose unadjusted hypothesis families, and label post-hoc logistic transitions and baseline comparisons exploratory. An interval containing an exponent establishes compatibility, not equivalence or a universal learning law.
- [ ] **Keep hash-stamped records byte-exact.** Reproduce writers in a disposable copy, preserve append-only run-log prefixes, and run `python tools/verify_studies.py`. Independent statistics and completed-Q diagnostics in `exploratory/exploratory_codex_2026-10-15/` read existing results without changing them. All six Q criteria were met, but DQN's fully charged controls often gambled; small treatment-control gaps are not evidence of no gambling. The exploratory DP-free rollout extension reports wide Monte Carlo uncertainty and transfer failures; it does not establish a general scalable predictor.
