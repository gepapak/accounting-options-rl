# Results in detail

A narrative summary of every study, kept from the repository's earlier README and extended. The paper and its supplement are the reference; each study's `VERDICTS_*` file is the registered record.

## The question
An accounting rule decides what an agent's owner receives once losses exceed equity, and that claim is an option. Trading simulators often floor an account's cash at zero, end an episode at bankruptcy, or let losses accumulate until a final settlement; real settlement systems book losses with a lag and hold collateral. Limited liability rewards risk, most strongly near zero equity ("gambling for resurrection"). This repository asks which option each rule writes, whether reinforcement-learning (RL) agents *learn* to exercise it, whether standard evaluation then reports the behavior as profit, whether acquisition can be anticipated, how to prevent it when the simulator's ledger is not exposed, and how large the option is in real Nordic imbalance settlement.

## Main results
- **Five accounting rules form a ladder of options.** Full liability ≤ capped final loss ≤ settlement lag ≤ terminal limited liability (Merton's call) ≤ the zero floor. In a market without edge, for fixed positions, zero costs and independent symmetric moves, the floor's subsidy is between one and two times the terminal call, reaching two in the diffusion limit (adaptive policies can exceed the factor two); reported equity is the booked ledger reflected at zero (for a constant position from zero equity with exchangeable increments it has the law of the running maximum), and reported equity alone cannot identify booked performance. Charging every injection restores the booked objective with continued trading.
- **Origin.** In an audit of a validated electricity-market engine, shortcuts from its earlier protocols ranked a learned controller first, reporting **+162% to +795%** in all six runs while its booked P&L was **−1,822% to −3,655%** (volume payoff). A missing liquidity cap and missing solvency rules were jointly necessary; reconciling reported against booked returns flagged 53 of 54 affected runs and none of 246 others. **Correction (6 November 2026):** the audit's legacy protocol L7 (+270% reported, −535% booked) was described as using a capped percentage payoff, but the cap was off in every run, because evaluation restores the checkpoint's saved configuration. With the cap on, MAPPO reports −38.5% and −45.0% against −7.9% and −13.7% for the rule, and the floor binds in 2 of 20 runs. See the engine section of the manuscript and the research record in `exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/engine_twin/`.
- **Agents learn to exercise the options** (placebo markets with sign-randomized imbalance spreads, where no policy can profit):
  - floor-trained agents raised leverage as equity fell and reported **+38% to +148%** while booking **−15% to −57%** (Study B; replicated with growing false profit in Study C);
  - with equity as the only input, **all ten floor-trained PPO agents chose maximal leverage at the low-equity probe**, 11.1 units of 16 above full-liability agents (Cohen's d = 2.6; Study F); A2C and DQN also gambled (Study I); Finland reproduced the pattern and calm southern Norway did not (Study J);
  - charging injections in full cut low-equity leverage by 9.6 units [4.5, 13.8] with matched continued trading (Study L);
  - under a **settlement lag, all ten forgiven-deficit agents took maximal leverage at every insolvent probe** (Study N, 6 of 9 hypotheses).
- **Anticipating acquisition (Studies O, P, Q; R failed).** A pre-training advantage-to-noise index, with a threshold fixed before Study O, classified 5 of 6 confident treatments (O 4/4); the threshold fell with training budget as N^−0.43 [−0.63, −0.22] (P 3/4; four times more training found an exploit missed at one million steps); the ordering transferred to A2C and DQN (Q 6/6). Simple cost-aware scores rank the arms equally, and Study R's prospective cost-transfer prediction failed (R 0/1). A reanalysis with the exploit's shape L(0.02) − L(2) confirms the orderings (`exploratory_claude_2026-10-16/reanalysis_shape/`).
- **Preventing the exploit without the ledger (Study S, 180 agents, 8/8 registered hypotheses supported).** The sign-paired reward clones the simulator before each step, reverses the exogenous sign in the clone, and trains on (r - r_twin)/2 - own cost. It recovers expected booked reward under conditional sign fairness and preserves booked reward when neither branch receives credit. Uniqueness is within a constant-coefficient linear class; with an edge and credits it can attenuate legitimate returns. In the developed DK1 edge testbed, new-seed SPR PPO booked +22.6% of capital per week (ledger training +18.1%) and avoided maximal leverage at all saved low-equity probes. None of three tested KL strengths met both registered prevention criteria. In the zero-floor fair-market test, SPR reduced exploit shape for PPO and A2C and met the registered five-unit noninferiority criterion against ledger training. DQN is not claimed (exploratory failure). The exploratory theory note is retained unchanged in `exploratory_claude_2026-10-16/nfl_training/`; see `REVIEW_ERRATA_2026-10-29.md` there and manuscript Section 8 (Propositions 4-7) and its supplement for the corrected statements and proofs.
- **Recovering the ledger from the reward channel (Studies T and U, 250 agents, 22/22 registered hypotheses supported).** Under the zero floor, and under the other rules of the ladder at their crediting steps (except the zombie window of a settlement lag), the reported reward is the booked profit censored from below by a hidden threshold, `r = max(booked, tau)`; a simulator that floors several cash writes in sequence is outside this form. A sign twin (the same step with the exogenous sign reversed) identifies the booked profit exactly where the favourable branch is uncensored (`r != r'`), brackets it where both branches are censored, and cannot do better where a carried deficit hides it (worst-case regret 4/9 per decision, verified by a linear program). The twin-identified reward (TIR) is exact where the twin identifies the profit and uses the sharp lower end of the identified set for the gross magnitude elsewhere, with pathwise error never above SPR's; SPR's half-difference is a valid but generally non-sharp lower bound (g = 10, C = 1, tau = 0 gives the pair (9, 0): the pair identifies g = 10, SPR uses 4.5). Exact rational checks: 200,000 random cases in `exploratory_claude_2026-10-31/identification/`, a 2,070-case audit in `exploratory_codex_2026-11-01/review_theory/` and an identified-set search in `exploratory_claude_2026-11-02/`. On a new DK1 market draw (Study T Part A) reported-reward PPO chose maximal leverage at 60% of low-equity probes and booked +2.0% of capital per week against +20.5% for ledger training, SPR 0% and +19.4%, TIR 2.5% and +21.3%; none of five ORPO strengths (occupancy-measure regularization, implemented from the authors' code semantics) did both (strong strengths reproduce the reference policy's +7.7%, weak ones regain the exploit). The result held in DK2 and in a weaker-edge DK1 setting. In Study N's settlement-lag and terminal-liability environments (Study U) both rewards brought acquisition down to the ledger control's level. TIR's exactness did not give a measurable learned advantage over SPR. The estimators are packaged and tested in `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/twin_reward/`.
- **Cheaper remedies, a reward without a clone, and a ledger-free audit (Study V, 144 agents, 14/17 registered hypotheses supported; VA3, VA5 and VC6 not supported).** A lump-sum ruin penalty (`RUIN`) and the quadratic-utility reward `r - lambda r^2` (`MV`, registered as "mean-variance") were compared with the twin rewards, together with the pooled-magnitude reward (PMR), which needs no clone: on favourable steps (r + cost)/|q| is the per-unit move when the favourable branch is uncensored, and PMR rewards sigma·|q|·(running mean of these) − cost (Assumption M: the move law does not depend on equity, action or sign). Reported-reward PPO gambled in 15 of 16 agents (Parts A and C); PMR removed the exploit in 31 of 32 agents (one Part A agent gambled, so VA3's edge bound, 0.48 against 0.60, failed) and booked 18.0%, 22.0%, 35.1% and 15.1% against 20.9%, 19.8%, 32.7% and 11.6% for ledger training; no quadratic-utility weight kept the edge (+8.6%, +4.5%, +1.1%); **a ruin penalty of 5% of the allocation removed the exploit and kept 91% (Part A) and 87% (Part C, half of the training episodes starting at zero equity) of the ledger edge, so VA5 and VC6 were not supported** (strength 1 left the gamble, strength 25 stopped trading from zero equity). Summing the TIR estimate over each agent's test episodes recovered the booked return of all 128 audited agents within 0.39 points; the reported return was off by a median of 68 points for the 28 agents that gambled. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_v/` and `exploratory_claude_2026-10-31/study_v_development/` (pilots, theory note).
- **Choosing the penalty's strength from the reward channel (Study W, 48 agents, 5/6 registered decisions supported; W4 not supported).** In two further edge-market settings (DK2 and a weaker-edge DK1 sleeve) the ruin-penalty strength with the highest TIR-audited return was the strength with the highest booked return (strength 5 in DK2, strength 1 in the weaker-edge sleeve), and it did both (102% and 96% of the ledger-trained edge, no probe at maximal leverage). Study W's W2 judges a strength by arm means; under the common criterion of Studies S to Y, which adds a one-sided Mann-Whitney test, strength 1 in DK2 does not remove the exploit (p = 0.076), while the two selected strengths meet both (`exploratory_claude_2026-11-03/w_common_criterion.py`, `study_w/ERRATA_W.md`). The TIR audit error was below 0.08 points for all 48 agents. The reported return would have chosen the same strength (W4 not supported, as registered beforehand), so in these settings the audit supplies the level of the return, not a better choice. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_w/`.
- **Do the twin rewards survive a violated single-floor assumption? (Study X, 32 agents, 6/6 registered decisions supported).** The public engine writes its cash in several floored steps, so the single-floor assumption behind SPR and TIR fails there; the exact cost-shift test (`twin_reward.cost_shift_test`, which decides whether any single-floor world reproduces the twin pairs at shifted costs) flags it. In a two-write variant of the edge-market sleeve (the opening fee written first with its own floor; half of the training episodes from zero equity), reported-reward PPO gambled in 8 of 8 agents and booked -13.4% of the allocation per week against +18.5% for ledger training; SPR and TIR gambled in 0 of 16 and booked +20.7% and +18.2% (1.12 and 0.99 of the ledger edge). The TIR audit error was at most 0.0065 points for the 24 agents that did not gamble and off by a median of 1.4 points (edge market) for the 8 that did, where the reported return was off by a median of 27. Graceful degradation in one sleeve, one learner and one write order, not evidence that the assumption is unnecessary. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_x/`.
- **A second learner (Study Y, 80 agents, 12/13 registered decisions supported; YC1 not supported).** A2C (rollout 64, as in Studies I, Q and S) in the single-floor and the two-write sleeve, with half of the training episodes from zero equity: reported-reward A2C gambled in 5 of 8 agents in each sleeve and booked +5.1% and +2.6% of the allocation per week against +21.1% for ledger training; SPR and TIR gambled in 0 of 32 and kept 1.00 to 1.04 of the ledger edge; the TIR audit of the 48 ledger, SPR and TIR agents was off by at most 0.0025 points. Without zero starts no reported-reward A2C agent gambled (YC1, anticipated in the registration), so A2C acquired the exploit only when training visited insolvent accounts. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_y/`.
- **A ledger-free bracket that needs no single floor (exploratory, 3 November 2026).** If the accounting only forgives (credits >= 0), the twin pair brackets each step's booked P&L in `[2*SPR - r, r]` (`twin_reward.twin_bracket`), a sharp interval whose width is the credit paid on the pair. On the stored audits of Studies V, W, X and Y (304 agents, 60,800 test episodes, single and two-write floors, PPO and A2C) no booked return lay outside its bracket, and on the public engine's own cash-writing methods it covered the booked value in all six hand cases, of which TIR's point estimate is wrong in four (`exploratory_claude_2026-11-03/twin_bracket.py`). Its premises are credits that are non-negative at every step on both branches and an own cost known and equal on both: the settlement lag's zombie window and a charge on one branch only break it, and the width, which covers both branches, was 1.35 to 2.42 times the forgiveness realized on the path (`exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/bracket_limits.py`).
- **When the trainer cannot see where losses are floored (Study Z, 136 agents, 22/26 registered decisions supported).** Part A: two accounts with their own floors trade the same spread (both balances visible); Part B: one balance and a margin mode whose isolated margin limits a position's loss to its own cost plus 100 EUR/MWh (the floors are hidden from the trainer). Reported-reward PPO gambled in both (booked -18.3% and -19.0% of the allocation per week against +33.8% and +20.6% for ledger training). **The ruin penalty measured on the portfolio or the balance did not remove the exploit at strength 5 or 25**; measured per account it did (79% of the edge), and a liquidation penalty, which needs the margin rule, did at 36% of the edge. SPR and TIR, from the portfolio's reward channel, removed it and kept 1.15 and 1.07 (Part A) and 0.99 and 1.02 (Part B) of the ledger-trained edge. A ledger-free finder, an agent rewarded by the twin bracket's width, found the hidden isolated-margin channel in all eight seeds. The bracket held on every audited step of all 136 agents. Not supported: ZA10 (pooled control, X 0.15 against 0.10), ZB3 (park share 0.47 against 0.50), ZIB2 (TIR audit 1.45 points, from the finder agents; 0.29 for the other 56, see `study_zb/ERRATA_ZB.md`) and ZIB3. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_z/` and `study_zb/`.
- **Real signs (Study RS, 80 agents, 6/8 registered decisions supported; RS3 and RS6 not).** A stress test of the twin rewards with the real DK2 spread, sign and magnitude, and a signal from the sign eight panel rows earlier (exactly two hours on 99.8% of test rows); the zero floor, the sign-reversed payoff and equal costs stay constructed. Reported-reward PPO gambled at 83% of the low-equity probes and booked -100.6% of the allocation per week while reporting +9.2%, against +8.5% booked for ledger training. SPR and TIR chose maximal leverage at no probe. **TIR kept 0.997 of the ledger-trained edge (bootstrap lower bound 0.755) and met the registered rule; SPR kept 0.687 (0.370) and did not;** a ruin penalty of 5 met the probe tolerance but booked -9.5%. This is a threshold contrast, not a shown superiority: TIR minus SPR is +2.64 points, exploratory 95% interval -0.87 to +6.29, Welch p = 0.18. The market, the lag and the zero starts were chosen after pilots scored on the same test panel, the 100 test windows overlap and hold about 31 separate weeks, and the run log's registered digest covers only its first 147 bytes through a hashing bug (`study_rs/README.md`). See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_rs/` and `exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/rs_reanalysis.py`.
- **The twin on the engine's own paths (exploratory, 6 November 2026; no agent trained).** A read-only tracer of every cash write re-evaluated the engine audit's frozen controllers: the 54 runs in which the floor bound and 39 in which it did not, each reproducing the audit's return within 1e-12 points, under rules hashed after three pilot runs. The twin is a one-step replay of the cash writes, in the engine's order, with the gross reversed. **The bracket contained the booked P&L on all 3,655,923 steps, and the reported return minus the summed TIR estimate flagged the same 53 of 54 runs as the ledger's reconciliation and none of the other 39.** Neither point estimate was safe: with the volume payoff TIR erred by a median of 43 points and SPR by 618, with the percentage payoff TIR by 608 and SPR by 11. Two of seven rules failed narrowly. A direct calculation was exact with the formula in force and off by medians of 1,925 and 838 points with the other protocol generation's. The engine exposes its position and gross P&L, so this tests the bracket on realistic accounting and does not show that a twin is needed there. See `exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/engine_twin/`.
- **The reward diagram (theory with exact checks, 6 November 2026).** Freeze a step on a clone and move the market input along a line, `input(t) = reference + t * (input - reference)`: `t = 1` is the real step, `t = -1` the sign twin, `t = 0` no move. For an accounting built from floored cash writes the reward is the booked P&L plus a convex non-negative credit, so it is the payoff diagram of the option book the accounting hands the agent: a kink is a floor, a flat tail reads the balance or the margin, opposite tail slopes are accounts of opposite exposure, a concave kink is a cap, a jump is a reward replaced on a branch. A zero width at scale `T` certifies that nothing is forgiven on `[-T, T]`. The endpoint estimator at range `L` has exact minimax error `(E + A + 2 eta) / L`, so the resource is the range and not the number of queries. Three witnesses show what the whole line cannot separate. `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/twin_reward/reward_diagram.py` is the reader, and `test_reward_diagram.py` runs 88,702 checks in rational arithmetic. See also `exploratory_claude_2026-11-06/reward_diagram/` (what it reads in every study sleeve) and `sign_cost/` (a one-sided certificate that needs no clone).
- **The reading on real clones of the engine (exploratory, 7 November 2026; no agent trained).** 26 evaluations of the engine audit's frozen controllers, every query being the engine's full, unchanged step on a deep copy, under rules hashed after four pilot runs: 170,326 read steps and 674,523 clones. **With the volume payoff (10 runs) the reading recovered the booked return within 0.0001 points at 2.5 to 5.1 clones per step, where audits from the same clones erred by 9 to 390 points (TIR) and 31 to 1,410 (SPR).** With the uncapped percentage payoff it ties SPR (13 to 18 points summed over read steps; TIR 826 to 1,228). A concave kink at `|t| <= 2` appears on 98.5 to 99.4% of the moved steps with the cap on and at most 0.6% with it off, so the reward alone separates the two protocols that the audit confused. **Eight of twelve rules were met.** Not met: S1 (two liquidation steps differ from the replay), S2 (the bracket misses on 5,278 steps, by at most 485 DKK, all where the own cost differs between the branches), S6 (capped payoff with the floor: 4.8 and 7.3 points against a limit of 1, SPR 0.2 and 0.3) and S9 (the flat level equals minus the balance within 1 DKK on only 87%). The reading needs the own cost declared: read from `t = 0` it errs by 188 to 882 points, as TIR and SPR would. Direct calculation stays exact, so this does not show that a reading is needed on this engine. See `exploratory_claude_2026-11-06/engine_scan/`.
- **An unmodified public simulator (Study EXT, 70 agents, 16/17 registered decisions supported; EA9 not).** The official Gym-Trading-Env at a pinned commit, byte-identical and checked at import, with a linear reward through its custom-reward function, positions up to +-10 and a placebo market of real daily Bitcoin moves with fair signs. On a step that takes the valuation to zero or below the engine ends the episode and does not call the reward function. **Reported-reward PPO chose maximal leverage at 89% of probes and went bust in 61% of test windows, reporting +50.4% per episode while booking -9.8%, against 3% and 2% on the public wealth.** SPR, TIR and TSR removed it; ruin penalties of 5 and 25 did not; one of 100 cut it to 18%, above the registered limit (three of eight seeds kept gambling). The finder chose maximal leverage in 8 of 8 seeds, and its negative control in the default position set had width 1e-13. The tail reading was exact on all 8,000 states and all 3,505 jumps were located and sized from the reward. The simulator exposes its wealth, so this shows that the exploit and the remedies carry over, not that a twin is needed. With the default logarithmic reward (exploratory, eight seeds, `study_ext/results_log/`) agents stayed nearly flat (X at most 0.011, no bust), so here the exploit needs the linear reward as well as the leverage. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_ext/`.
- **A correct copy of the reward's definition certifies the floor (exploratory, 7 November 2026; no agent trained).** On unmodified gym-mtsim 2.0.0 (EURUSD, 100 held positions, 11 probes each at +-1, 2, 4, 8, 16 times a 1% move, seed 20261107) the reward is defined as the change in equity, and a reimplementation of that definition matched the returned reward on all 1,100 probes while the zero-balance write in `tick()` hid 11,587 to 11,846 USD of the order's loss at the -16% probe. The slope of the reward around a zero move recovers the exposure (relative error 1e-14) and the economic profit to 1.6e-10 USD; a control subclass that deletes the two floor lines passes 100 of 100 probes. See `exploratory_line_2026-11-07/` (`PROTOCOL.md`, `run_formula_gap.py`, `results.json`).
- **Logs do not buy range (proposition with a witness, 7 November 2026).** Two floored programs that agree on `[-lambda, lambda]` write identical transition logs of any length whose moves lie inside it, so no estimator from such logs separates them, and one query outside does. Witness on the same gym-mtsim positions: 5,000 logged moves inside 0.4% are identical with and without the floor lines, an OLS line through them recovers the exposure (relative error 5e-16) and, extrapolated to the -16% probe, misses the public reward by 11,827 USD while matching the unfloored control to 7e-12 USD. See `exploratory_line_2026-11-07/` (`PROTOCOL_RANGE.md`, `run_range_logs.py`, `results_range.json`).
- **A two-action learner on the same simulator (exploratory, 8 November 2026).** Flat or the margin maximum, equity 10,000 USD, one bar, 1,000 Student-t(3) draws and 20 seeds. The line through the published reward at a zero move matches the floor-deleted step within 1.5e-9 USD. At 0.15% per bar both learners stay flat. At 1.2% the published mean exceeds that line by 504 to 750 USD on 20 of 20 seeds. A pre-registered count of 18 commits to the maximum was not met (16 of 20). See `exploratory_mtsim_reach_2026-11-08/` (`PROTOCOL_LEARN.md`).
- **Reach before training (Study RE, 50 PPO runs, 3/3 registered decisions supported).** Scaling the Study F sleeve's spreads and fee by `c` in {1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01} moves only how often training can reach the floor. At `c = 0.01` the predicted reach is 0, no run hit the floor, and all five floored/unfloored pairs have identical probes and identical parameter digests. At full scale the exploit gap averages 13.2 units of leverage. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_re/`.
- **Accountings that a sign query cannot read (Study FT, 81 agents, 20/23 registered decisions supported; FB4, FB6 and FB7 not).** The edge-market sleeve with an unrealized bucket (Part A), a carried deficit with half of the training funded (Part B) and a carried deficit only (Part C). Reported-reward PPO gambled in all three (X 0.98, 0.33, 0.75; from a deficit -30.6%, -1.3%, -27.3% per week against about +20.6%). **SPR and TIR removed the exploit everywhere, but what they kept differs: 0.80 and 0.84 of the edge with the bucket, 0.01 and 0.002 with a deficit and no funded training (the study's main prediction), and 0.55 and 0.58 with half funded, where our prediction from one pilot seed failed.** TSR matched the ledger arm exactly on each of its three seeds, as it must by construction. From a deficit the twin audits err by about 30 points per week on honest agents and the reading by at most 1.1e-12. See `ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/study_ft/`.
- **Model selection.** In an independent certification cohort, the winners selected on reported return (+108.8% and +104.3%) were certified worst in expected booked return within their ten-agent sets, with one joint 95% guarantee (Study M).
- **Historical settlement-component stress test (exploratory).** A proxy for the historical eSett volume term was below a modeled three-week deterrence buffer in **36.8%** of post-March-2025 FI/SE/NO zone-months versus **8.6%** before. It excludes the invoice component, minimum collateral and monitoring, and applying the historical formula to Finland after November 2024 and to Denmark is counterfactual; it is not an assessment of total collateral adequacy or conduct. The fee-free floor/terminal ratio was 1.62–1.99 across 24 zone-years.
- **Boundaries.** With realistic trading costs (Study H), inside gym-mtsim (Study G) and with equity-scaled positions in our sleeve (Study E), agents did not learn to gamble, or did so only weakly. That scope is not general: in Gym-Trading-Env, whose positions scale with wealth, leaving the bankruptcy step's loss unrewarded sufficed (Study EXT). Reported returns still exceed booked ones wherever a floor binds.
- **Prevalence.** Two of the 18 most-starred public RL trading environments forgive losses: one by default with orders of fixed size, the other only when users enable shorts or leverage (Study D); 20 of 21 public electricity-RL environments have no capital account.
- **Tools.** Reconciliation of reported against booked returns, a placebo-market test, simultaneous cost-based certificates, the reader of the reward diagram (`ACCOUNTING_OPTIONS_2026-10-14/twin_reward/reward_diagram.py`), the sign-paired and twin-identified rewards, the exact cost-shift test of their single-floor assumption (`twin_reward/`; a falsification test, not a certificate) and an evaluation checklist (`CHECKLIST.md`). Dated errata for statements in hashed protocols: `study_t/ERRATA_T.md`, `study_w/ERRATA_W.md`, `study_x/ERRATA_X.md`, `study_zb/ERRATA_ZB.md` (the protocols are unchanged); Study RS's registration defect is described in `study_rs/README.md`. Analyses added after the fourth AI-assisted review (audit cohorts, Study W under both criteria, Holm sensitivity, validation of the exact test) and the Study RS reanalysis are in `exploratory_claude_2026-11-03/` and `exploratory_claude_2026-11-05/`.

## Studies added from 14 to 17 November 2026
- **gym-mtsim's floor in learning (Study GC, 40 PPO agents, 6/7 registered decisions supported; GC7 not).** In the package's own crypto market the floor wrote in about three quarters of the training episodes; floor-trained agents opened 0.28 of the margin-feasible maximal position at low equity against 0.08 with the two floor lines deleted (difference 0.20, 95% interval 0.10 to 0.29) and reported 3.36 points above booked; agents trained on the reading of the published reward did not learn it (0.05). In the forex market no agent traded on the test paths. GC7 (the reading exact to 0.001 USD on every step) failed on one step in 11.4 million, where the account lay closer to the floor than the reading's price bump (`study_gc_2026-11-14/gc7_diagnosis.txt`). See `study_gc_2026-11-14/`.
- **A widened training distribution (Study DR, 64 PPO agents, 1/4 registered decisions supported).** Randomizing the forex market's volatility raised low-equity exposure with the range (Jonckheere p = 0.035), but leverage 500 did not switch the floor on (2 learners of 10) and the narrowest range, predicted dormant, had learners in 3 of 6 runs: the pre-training one-bar gain failed as a predictor in both directions. - **Mainstream backtesting engines (Study XE, 56 PPO agents, 4/7 registered decisions supported; XE2, XE3, XE7 not).** backtesting.py 0.6.6 sets equity to zero and stops at the first bar close with equity at or below zero; backtrader 1.9.78 books the loss.
